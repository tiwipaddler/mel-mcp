# Why Kenyan agriculture projects reported delays or missed targets since 2020, and whether the indicators show it

*Evidence summary from the MEL evidence base (search index and indicator tracker both refreshed 2026-11-23). Filters: country = Kenya, year from 2020.*

**Short answer:** The reasons given most often were slow procurement and payments, weak implementing capacity and coordination, and slow start-up or poor sequencing. One World Bank project was badly delayed by land acquisition. COVID-19 and other shocks also appear. The indicator tracker only partly shows the shortfalls. Only 3 of the 9 cited projects have usable tracker data, all from the African Development Bank (AfDB). The reports confirmed 6 of the 12 tracker figures checked. In three cases the tracker showed a target as met or exceeded when the reports show a shortfall.

**Notes on citations:** None of the documents give a sample size, so every citation reads "n not reported". The phases are as the reports label them; "other" mostly means a progress report.

## 1. Themes, with Kenya examples

### T1. Slow procurement, weak financial management and late payments
- **Kenya Small-scale Irrigation & Value Addition (AfDB)**, Project Completion Report, p.13–14 (endline, 2026; n not reported). Slow procurement and weak contract management caused delays. Audits found payments made before works were certified complete. The report says these problems "contributed to the underachievement of some key targets."
- **Kenya Water Security & Climate Resilience Project, P117635 (World Bank)**, Implementation Completion and Results Report, p.26 (endline, 2025; n not reported). Payments were delayed through the National Treasury and two parallel funding streams. Contractors ran short of cash, filed claims, and timelines were extended.

### T2. Weak implementing capacity and coordination (national, county, between agencies)
- **AEFPF-K food production facility (AfDB)**, Project Completion Report, p.12 (endline, 2024; n not reported). Signing and start-up were delayed during the change of government. Coordination between the National Treasury and the Ministry of Agriculture was difficult.
- **Small-scale Irrigation (AfDB)**, Project Completion Report, p.13 (endline, 2026; n not reported). National and county capacity was limited, and coordination across counties was "suboptimal."
- **GW4R groundwater programme (FCDO 300808)**, evaluation report, June 2026 (other, 2026; n not reported). Overly complex institutions and poor coordination meant the first construction began only three years into the programme. *This is a water programme, not agriculture.*

### T3. Land acquisition and compensation
- **Water Security project (World Bank)**, Implementation Completion Report Review, p.31 (endline, 2026; n not reported). Land acquisition was not identified as a risk at design and delayed the project by two years.
- **Same project**, Implementation Completion and Results Report, p.26 (endline, 2025; n not reported). Disputes over compensation and only a two-month window between cropping seasons slowed construction.
- *This comes from one project only, so it is not a pattern.*

### T4. Slow start-up, design and sequencing
- **AgriGRADE (Netherlands)**, annual report 2024, p.64 and p.24 (other, 2025; n not reported). The inception phase took 6 months instead of 2. Because steps run in sequence, the delays compounded. *The project covers Kenya and Tanzania, and this passage doesn't say which country.*
- **Enable Youth (AfDB)**, progress report June 2025, p.3 (other, 2025; n not reported). Start-up delays, problems with the incubation and training consultancy, and a project suspension.
- **CASA smallholder programme (FCDO 205118)**, annual review Feb 2026 (monitoring, 2026; n not reported). Kenya's first-year underspend was partly due to start-up delays.

### T5. Outside shocks (COVID-19, supply problems, a donor budget cut, weather)
- **Enable Youth (AfDB)**, progress reports 2024–25 (other; n not reported). Incubation and training were delayed by COVID-19.
- **Mount Kenya III irrigation (KfW)**, ex-post evaluation, p.17 (ex-post, 2020; n not reported). COVID closed markets and some harvests rotted. *Most of this evaluation's data are from 2019.*
- **AEFPF-K (AfDB)**, progress report Nov 2024, p.4 (other, 2024; n not reported). Sunflower planting was delayed by imports.
- **CASA (FCDO)**, annual review June 2021 (monitoring, 2021; n not reported). An output was missed because of FCDO's budget cut in September 2020. *The programme covers several countries.*
- Weather and climate appear in these documents mainly as risks in risk registers (AEFPF-K, AgriGRADE), not as the stated cause of a missed target.

### T6. Input-delivery design and gaps in monitoring data
- **AEFPF-K (AfDB)**, Project Completion Report, p.13 (endline, 2024; n not reported). Leaving private agro-dealers out made fertilizer distribution less efficient and less well targeted.
- **AEFPF-K (AfDB)**, progress report Nov 2024, p.5 (other, 2024; n not reported). Several indicators are marked "No data reported."
- **Water Security project (World Bank)**, Completion Report Review, p.10 (endline, 2026; n not reported). Physical progress was poorly measured, and the indicators "remain output-oriented."

## 4. Cited projects with no usable tracker figures

The World Bank Water Security project is in the tracker, but none of its 25 indicators is usable: each period has a placeholder end date of 1900, and target and actual are 0 or missing (nothing was reported). The other projects below returned no tracker indicators when searched by name or code. These are the figures their own reports state:

- **Water Security & Climate Resilience (World Bank, P117635).** Completion Report Review p.10–11 (endline, 2026):
  - 2,457 ha irrigated, 79% of the revised target and 60% of the original.
  - 8,142 farmers reached, 73% of the revised target.
  - Crops marketed worth $7.3m against $11.37m (Completion Report p.66, 64%). The original target was $30m.
- **AgriGRADE (Netherlands).** Annual report 2024 p.8: 56 of 90 selected farmer organisations assessed in 2024. *This covers Kenya and Tanzania combined.*
- **CASA (FCDO 205118).** Annual review Feb 2026: CASA Kenya "met or exceeded all output targets."
- **Mount Kenya III (KfW).** Ex-post evaluation p.9 (2020, data from 2019):
  - 561 ha irrigated against 544 ha designed.
  - Cropping intensity of 201–202% against a 220% target ("almost fulfilled").
- **GW4R groundwater (FCDO 300808).** No achievement-against-target figures found.

## 5. How strong the evidence is

- **Coverage:** 3 of 9 cited projects had usable tracker data, all AfDB. The reports confirmed 6 of the 12 tracker figures. Three disagreed with the reports, and in each case the tracker overstated progress. Three could not be checked.
- **Reasons (themes): moderate.** They come up again and again in endline reports from several donors, but most of the detail is from AfDB and the World Bank. These are donors' own reports, with no beneficiary data and no sample sizes.
- **Size of the shortfalls: limited.** Only the World Bank water project (independently reviewed) and AEFPF-K have clear endline figures, and AEFPF-K's own reports contradict each other. Elsewhere the tracker either can't be checked or overstates achievement.
- **Gaps:**
  - No focus groups, interviews or surveys turned up, so there is nothing on how farmers experienced the delays.
  - No figures broken down by gender, youth or county.
  - Several other donors don't appear in these results, including IFAD, the EU, USAID and FAO. GIZ's Western Kenya projects are in the index but didn't come up.
  - The only ex-post evaluation (KfW) mostly covers years before 2020.
  - The places covered are the ASAL (arid and semi-arid) counties, Lower Nzoia/Busia, Makueni/Kitui and Mount Kenya; northern Kenya appears only through the water programme.
