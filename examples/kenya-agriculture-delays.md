# Kenyan agriculture projects since 2020: why they were late or missed targets, and whether the indicators show it

> Example answer from the [Kenya starter pack](../starter/kenya-agriculture/README.md), asked in Claude Code on 26 November 2026 with the question below. Answers vary a little between runs. Every citation links to the report, at the page cited, so each finding can be checked.
>
> *What are the most common reasons Kenyan agriculture projects reported delays or missed targets since 2020, and do the indicators show the shortfalls?*

*Evidence base: the MEL report index (built 2026-11-26) and the IATI indicator tracker (updated 2026-11-26). All documents cited are programme reports (AfDB IPRs and PCRs, World Bank ICR and ICR Review, FCDO annual reviews). None reports a sample size (n), because none is a survey.*

---

## Short answer

1. **Five causes come up repeatedly:**
   - slow procurement, weak fiduciary and financial management, and late payments
   - land acquisition and compensation, together with safeguard documents that weren't ready
   - late effectiveness or suspension, often tied to political cycles
   - COVID-19 and other external shocks: floods, and inflation in input prices
   - donor budget or start-up problems that slowed partnership deals
2. **The indicators show some of these shortfalls, but not reliably.** Where the tracker has usable figures, the large shortfalls (Enable Youth, AEFPF-K fertilizer and finance) agree with the reports. Several tracker figures differ from the reports, though:
   - One overstates achievement by counting registered farmers as beneficiaries.
   - Some show the target copied into the actual.
   - One project, the Water Security and Climate Resilience Project, has **no usable figures at all**.

---

## 1. Common reasons for delays and missed targets

| Reason | Projects reporting it | Evidence (phase) |
|---|---|---|
| **Procurement, fiduciary and payment delays** | Small-scale Irrigation & Value Addition (AAZ-009); Water Security & Climate Resilience (P117635) | AAZ-009: "Financial management and procurement processes were marred by delays and irregularities… Procurement cycles were slow, and contract management was weak" ([PCR p.13](https://www.afdb.org/sites/default/files/documents/projects-and-operations/kenya-smallscale_irrigation_agriculture_value_chain_development-_p-ke-aaz-009-pcr-octobre_2026.pdf#page=13), endline 2026). P117635: weak procurement and contract-management capacity led to "lengthy delays and contractual claims" ([ICR p.30](https://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/099122425102515384/pdf/BOSIB-24e62aba-6021-41bf-82e8-bc2d425ba9ef.pdf#page=30), endline 2025). Funds flowing through the National Treasury and parallel WB/KfW funding left contractors short of cash ([ICR p.26](https://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/099122425102515384/pdf/BOSIB-24e62aba-6021-41bf-82e8-bc2d425ba9ef.pdf#page=26)). |
| **Land acquisition, compensation and safeguards** | P117635 | The Land Act 2012 process is complex. Compensation payments alone took 3–4 months, and government funding for compensation fell short ([ICR p.26](https://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/099122425102515384/pdf/BOSIB-24e62aba-6021-41bf-82e8-bc2d425ba9ef.pdf#page=26)). The project had no specific funding for crop compensation, which delayed construction ([ICR p.33](https://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/099122425102515384/pdf/BOSIB-24e62aba-6021-41bf-82e8-bc2d425ba9ef.pdf#page=33)). The ICR Review lists poor feasibility studies, procurement, land acquisition, safeguards and complex arrangements as the causes inside the project's control ([ICRR p.28](https://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/099051526122584470/pdf/P117635-9bf7728d-7c1c-44c3-ba0a-c460082a1291.pdf#page=28), [p.36](https://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/099051526122584470/pdf/P117635-9bf7728d-7c1c-44c3-ba0a-c460082a1291.pdf#page=36), endline 2026). |
| **Late effectiveness, suspension and political cycles** | AEFPF-K (A00-002); Enable Youth (AAZ-014) | AEFPF-K: the government signed the loan agreement **10 months late** because of the political transition after the 2022 election ([PCR p.7](https://www.afdb.org/sites/default/files/documents/projects-and-operations/kenya-_african_emergency_food_production_facility-kenya_aefpf-k-_p-ke-a00-002-pcr-november_2024.pdf#page=7), [p.13](https://www.afdb.org/sites/default/files/documents/projects-and-operations/kenya-_african_emergency_food_production_facility-kenya_aefpf-k-_p-ke-a00-002-pcr-november_2024.pdf#page=13), endline 2024). That left about one year to implement, too short to set up the fertilizer financing mechanism ([PCR p.2](https://www.afdb.org/sites/default/files/documents/projects-and-operations/kenya-_african_emergency_food_production_facility-kenya_aefpf-k-_p-ke-a00-002-pcr-november_2024.pdf#page=2)). Enable Youth: "significant delays due [to] incubation and training challenges, COVID 19 pandemic, and project suspension" ([IPR Jun 2025 p.3](https://www.afdb.org/sites/default/files/documents/projects-and-operations/kenya-_enable_youth_kenya-_p-ke-aaz-014-ipr-june_2025.pdf#page=3), monitoring). |
| **COVID-19 and other external shocks** | P117635; Enable Youth; CASA | P117635: COVID-19 delayed supply chains and contractor mobilisation, and floods added to the delays ([ICRR p.28](https://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/099051526122584470/pdf/P117635-9bf7728d-7c1c-44c3-ba0a-c460082a1291.pdf#page=28); [ICR p.22](https://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/099122425102515384/pdf/BOSIB-24e62aba-6021-41bf-82e8-bc2d425ba9ef.pdf#page=22)). CASA: global food and input price inflation slowed partnership negotiations ([AR Aug 2024](http://iati.fcdo.gov.uk/iati_documents/D0005587.odt), monitoring). |
| **Donor budget uncertainty and slow start-up** | CASA (FCDO 205118, multi-country including Kenya) | Because FCDO deferred budgets, the programme couldn't sign partnership agreements ([AR Sep 2022](http://iati.fcdo.gov.uk/iati_documents/D0001230.odt)). Partnership sign-off took 4–9 months ([AR Aug 2024](http://iati.fcdo.gov.uk/iati_documents/D0005587.odt)). Year-1 underspend came from start-up delays and poor forecasts ([AR Feb 2026](http://iati.fcdo.gov.uk/iati_documents/S20511835.odt)). *These reviews cover several countries, so not every point is specific to Kenya.* |

**Pattern:** the reports describe most of these delays as **internal and administrative**: procurement, funds flow, land, and readiness of safeguard documents. External shocks (COVID-19, floods, elections, inflation) made them worse.

## 2. Do the indicators show the shortfalls?

### Kenya – Enable Youth (AAZ-014): yes, and the reports agree
[Activity page](https://d-portal.iatistandard.org/ctrack.html#view=act&aid=46002-P-KE-AAZ-014). The reporting period runs from 2018 to 2026, so these figures include results from before 2020.

| Indicator | Target | Actual | % |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jobs created | 8,000 | 800 | 10% |
| Business proposals approved for financing | 1,200 | 192 | 16% |
| Youth agribusinesses established | 1,200 | 200 | 17% |

The June 2025 IPR reports the same figures ([p.2–3](https://www.afdb.org/sites/default/files/documents/projects-and-operations/kenya-_enable_youth_kenya-_p-ke-aaz-014-ipr-june_2025.pdf#page=3)). **One caveat:** for several output indicators the tracker shows the actual equal to the target, for example 1,250 candidates trained in incubation and $50m leveraged through the Risk Guarantee Fund. The IPR shows **0% progress** on those same indicators ([p.4](https://www.afdb.org/sites/default/files/documents/projects-and-operations/kenya-_enable_youth_kenya-_p-ke-aaz-014-ipr-june_2025.pdf#page=4)). It looks like the target was copied into the actual, so these figures hide a shortfall.

### Kenya – AEFPF-K (A00-002): partly; one indicator overstates achievement
[Activity page](https://d-portal.iatistandard.org/ctrack.html#view=act&aid=46002-P-KE-A00-002). Period 2022–2025. The project published no baseline.

| Indicator | Tracker | What the reports say (preferred) |
|---|---|---|
| Fertilizer distributed by e-voucher | 293,442 of 600,000 t (49%) | Missed. The PCR gives 341,651 t, or 57% ([PCR p.5](https://www.afdb.org/sites/default/files/documents/projects-and-operations/kenya-_african_emergency_food_production_facility-kenya_aefpf-k-_p-ke-a00-002-pcr-november_2024.pdf#page=5), endline). Earlier, the April 2024 IPR gave 179,442 t ([p.6](https://www.afdb.org/sites/default/files/documents/projects-and-operations/kenya_-_african_emergency_food_production_facility_aefpf-k_-_p-ke-a00-002_-_ipr_april_2024.pdf#page=6)). The sources disagree, but every one shows a shortfall. |
| Farmers receiving certified seed | 3,228,491 of 1.3m (**248%**) | **Overstated.** 3,228,491 is the number of farmers *registered* on the e-voucher system. The PCR attributes only **389,080** beneficiaries to AEFPF-K, which it calls "way below the end-target" ([PCR p.5](https://www.afdb.org/sites/default/files/documents/projects-and-operations/kenya-_african_emergency_food_production_facility-kenya_aefpf-k-_p-ke-a00-002-pcr-november_2024.pdf#page=5)). For farmers receiving inputs, the PCR gives 389,080 of 600,000 (65%) ([p.7](https://www.afdb.org/sites/default/files/documents/projects-and-operations/kenya-_african_emergency_food_production_facility-kenya_aefpf-k-_p-ke-a00-002-pcr-november_2024.pdf#page=7)). |
| AFC financing to the private sector | $0 of $10m | Confirmed. The PCR says the financing component was "not implemented" because the implementation period was so short ([PCR p.2](https://www.afdb.org/sites/default/files/documents/projects-and-operations/kenya-_african_emergency_food_production_facility-kenya_aefpf-k-_p-ke-a00-002-pcr-november_2024.pdf#page=2)). |
| Farmers receiving extension through e-voucher | 0 of 600,000 | **Unknown.** The IPR says "No data reported", so the 0 means nothing was reported, not that nothing was delivered ([IPR p.5](https://www.afdb.org/sites/default/files/documents/projects-and-operations/kenya_-_african_emergency_food_production_facility_aefpf-k_-_p-ke-a00-002_-_ipr_april_2024.pdf#page=5)). |

### Kenya – Small-scale Irrigation & Value Addition (AAZ-009): broadly, but some 100% figures are doubtful
[Activity page](https://d-portal.iatistandard.org/ctrack.html#view=act&aid=46002-P-KE-AAZ-009). The period runs from 2015 to 2026, so the figures include results from before 2020.

- **New irrigation area:** the tracker shows 1,326 of 1,644 ha (81%). The PCR reports land development of 2,283 of 2,567 ha (89%) and 11 of 12 schemes completed ([PCR p.3](https://www.afdb.org/sites/default/files/documents/projects-and-operations/kenya-smallscale_irrigation_agriculture_value_chain_development-_p-ke-aaz-009-pcr-octobre_2026.pdf#page=3)). Both show a modest shortfall, but they measure different things.
- **Maize yield:** 14.4 t/ha against a target of 15, up from a baseline of 2.5 (96%). The PCR confirms this.
- **Doubtful 100% figures:** the tracker reports "Rain-fed area brought under irrigation" as 300 of 300 ha. The PCR's own description adds up to about 187 ha in 19 micro-irrigation schemes, plus 47.84 ha supported through other water infrastructure ([PCR p.6](https://www.afdb.org/sites/default/files/documents/projects-and-operations/kenya-smallscale_irrigation_agriculture_value_chain_development-_p-ke-aaz-009-pcr-octobre_2026.pdf#page=6)). The tracker also shows the same target and actual for this indicator and for erosion-control structures. Read the 100% as unconfirmed.
- The tracker figures hardly reflect the fiduciary and procurement delays the PCR describes, because most outputs were eventually delivered. Those problems show up as lost time and efficiency, not as missed output targets.

### Kenya Water Security & Climate Resilience Project (P117635): no usable indicator figures
[Activity page](https://d-portal.iatistandard.org/ctrack.html#view=act&aid=XI-IATI-WBTF-P117635). None of its 25 indicators is usable:
- every period has a placeholder end date of 1900
- most targets are 0
- several actuals are text rather than numbers
- two give implausible results (620% and 5,300% of target)

So the indicators **can't show** this project's shortfalls, even though it has the most detailed account of delays: seven restructurings, design revisions that took four years, cost overruns ([ICR p.22](https://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/099122425102515384/pdf/BOSIB-24e62aba-6021-41bf-82e8-bc2d425ba9ef.pdf#page=22)). Use the ICR results framework instead.

### CASA (FCDO 205118)
CASA has no Kenya indicators in the tracker, so only its annual reviews (narrative) cover it.

---

## 3. Bottom line

- **Where the indicators work, they confirm the large shortfalls:** Enable Youth reached 10–17% of its job and enterprise targets, and AEFPF-K's fertilizer and finance components fell well short.
- **They miss or distort some shortfalls:**
  - Copied targets on Enable Youth outputs and AAZ-009 micro-irrigation make results look complete.
  - AEFPF-K's seed beneficiaries are inflated about eightfold by counting registered farmers.
  - Some zeros mean "not reported", not "not achieved".
  - P117635 has no usable figures at all.
- **Delays matter more than missed outputs:** the main causes the reports describe (procurement, land and funds flow) show up mostly as time and cost overruns. Output indicators don't capture those, so check timelines and restructurings in the reports.
